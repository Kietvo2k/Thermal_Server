Your school's computers sleep 13 hours a night. We put them to work.

Thermal Orchestrator runs AI jobs on the PCs a school already owns, after the last class ends. It watches each machine's temperature, so jobs move away before a PC runs hot.

Schools have the data and the computers, but not the AI server

  • Data piles up every day. Camera footage, attendance, grades and enrolment forms are mostly checked by hand.
  • A GPU server is expensive. Around up front (our assumption) before any AI work starts.
  • Data should stay inside. Cloud AI means student data leaves the school network.

What runs at night

For schools

  • Camera reviewFlags crowded areas, people in restricted zones, left objects and unusual events.Safety limit: never decides a student broke a rule; a person checks the original video.
  • Attendance alertsCounts absences, spots unusual changes, lists students for the form teacher to check.Safety limit: never guesses the reason or disciplines anyone.
  • Learning analysisCompares results by class and subject, suggests groups that need help.Safety limit: never grades conduct or makes big decisions.
  • Form readingReads enrolment forms, finds missing fields, sorts documents.Safety limit: staff confirm the data first.

For clinics

  • Visitor flowCounts people in public areas, finds peak hours, estimates waiting time.Safety limit: never identifies patients.
  • Admin recordsSorts records, pulls out fields, flags missing information.Safety limit: staff confirm before use.
  • Medicine and suppliesWarns about items near expiry, fast drops in stock, and coming shortages.Safety limit: never orders or changes stock by itself.
  • Daily reportSums up check-ins, waiting time, records and events.Safety limit: never diagnoses or suggests treatment.
